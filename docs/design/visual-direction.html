Ctrl+Alt+Defend: visual direction

Mockup for validation, not the build. The world is the inside of a computer seen from above: a dark circuit board (solder mask, copper traces, white silkscreen labels) where the hardware is the terrain, your security programs glow cyan and green, viruses glow pink and violet, and your data glows yellow. Every color has one job, so the board reads at a glance. All numbers shown (bits, damage, waves) are placeholders.

3 towersFirewall (cheap wall; from v2.0 boosts the towers next to it), Antivirus (fast single shots), Debugger (ramping beam, beats armor).
2 enemiesWorm (fast, splits in two on death) and Rootkit (slow, armored).
Why this setAntivirus answers Worms, Debugger answers Rootkits, Firewall shapes the maze and makes neighbors stronger.
Upgrade labFirewall and Antivirus start unlocked; Debugger and every v2.0 / v3.0 are bought with keys.

Colors

Dark only. Five families, each with one job: the board is terrain, cyan and green are yours, pink and violet are threats, yellow is data you must protect. Red is reserved for loss and invalid actions. Every text color passes WCAG AA on the panel color.

Typography

Two families. Chakra Petch is squared and chip-like, for names and headings. B612 was drawn for aircraft cockpit screens: built to be read fast, small, on dark displays, which is what a match HUD is. Its mono cut sets every number so counters don't jitter.

Enemies

Threat colors only. Pink is fast and many; violet is slow and heavy. Hit feedback is a short chromatic glitch (with reduced motion: a quick fade instead). Health bars sit above the virus; Rootkit's bar carries a small armor plate. A carrier holds the yellow file above its body so you can always find your data.

Towers

One tower per cell, on a pad. The version shows as three pips along the bottom edge. Firewall is a solid wall; from v2.0 it boosts every tower sharing a side with it, shown as a steel chevron on the boosted tower and a bright edge where they touch. Antivirus turns its barrel toward its target; Debugger is a fixed emitter whose beam thickens as it ramps.

Build menu items

Map pieces

The hardware is the terrain. Obstacles are real board parts with silkscreen names, block both walking and building, and differ per level. Ports are edge connectors; the hatched keep-out strip inside each port can't be built on.
